The demo, word for word

Twelve stages, in order

Each stage sets up the next, so don't reorder them. The through-line is simple: an AI that is allowed to do anything is a liability, and an AI on a leash is an asset — and you can prove which one this is, live, without asking anyone to trust you.

You do not have to click all of this. Run cycle plays stages 1 to 6 on a timer and halts at the gate, leaving you free to talk over it. Use it when the room is short of time or you would rather not hunt for controls. Drive it by hand when someone is engaged and asking questions — the clicks are the proof.

  1. 01

    The money slide

    Do
    In the left column, click TRACK 1, then click TRACK 2.
    They see
    Every number on the dashboard repaints. Cost per unit of software delivered falls from €1,200 to €180. Delivery time drops from 14 days to 2.

    Let me start with the outcome, and then I'll show you how it's earned.

    These two views are the same team, the same client, the same piece of work. The only difference is how much control sits around the AI doing it.

    On the left, the traditional way of buying people by the hour. On the right, the same work delivered under full governance — and it costs about a seventh as much. That's the claim. Everything after this is me proving it isn't a slide.

  2. 02

    It refuses to start

    Do
    Click Context & Mandate on the left, then click Run probe. If the room is technical, follow it with View spine.
    They see
    An amber warning: agent initialisation held. Two problems named — an out-of-date tax table, and a customer billing file whose contents were never labelled. The big button on the right greys out.

    Before this AI is allowed to touch anything, it checks the information it's about to rely on. Not the code — the facts.

    If you opened the spine: this is every piece of information the AI may use, traced from the ticket that asked for the work through to the one file it may change. The dashed red line is the problem — that out-of-date file feeds the same place as everything else, so anything built on it inherits the mistake.

    And look what it found. The tax table it was going to use is out of date. And there's a file of customer billing data sitting in the pile that nobody ever labelled as sensitive — so nobody knows the phone numbers and tax IDs in it are exposed.

    Now, here is the part I actually want you to notice. It stopped. It will not start. The button is greyed out and I cannot click past it.

    This is the single most expensive failure in AI projects right now: a confident answer built on stale or unlicensed data, delivered fast, and wrong. Most tools would have carried on. This one refuses — and that refusal is the product.

  3. 03

    Fix it in front of them

    Do
    Click Fix with Avenga Intelligence, then Run Data Product Factory.
    They see
    A green confirmation. The tax table is rebuilt, the billing file is labelled as sensitive, and the quality score climbs back to 97%.

    Finding the problem is only half of it. This repairs it — rebuilds the out-of-date table, and labels that customer file as sensitive so everything downstream knows to treat it carefully.

    Ninety seconds, and the ground the AI stands on is solid. Now it's allowed to start.

  4. 04

    The permission slip

    Do
    Point at the three sliders and the file list on the right, then click Sign Mandate & initialise agent.
    They see
    The status badge flips from Unsigned to Active and starts pulsing.

    This is the permission slip. Before the AI does anything, somebody writes down exactly what it's allowed to do.

    How many attempts it gets. How much it's allowed to spend. When that permission expires — fifteen minutes, and then it's void. And this list here is the only part of the system it can reach. Everything crossed out is off limits: the payment systems, the production servers, the deployment pipeline. It cannot get to them. Not "shouldn't" — cannot.

    I'll sign it, and the AI starts work inside that box and nowhere else.

  5. 05

    Every action, checked

    Do
    Click Grounded Execution, then click Execute next tool-call four times, pausing between clicks.
    They see
    A notice slides in at the top right for each action. The record on the left grows. The before-and-after code appears on the right.

    Now it's working. Every single thing it does — reading a file, running a test, making a change — passes through a checkpoint first, and each one is logged as it happens.

    That panel on the left is the record building itself in real time. Nobody writes it afterwards, nobody can forget to write it, and nobody can quietly tidy it up later.

    On the right is the actual change. Old version, new version, side by side.

  6. 06

    The hard stop

    Do
    Click Request production merge.
    They see
    A full-screen amber gate: manual authorisation required, with the impact analysis on the left and the change on the right.

    Here's where it wants to put this change live — and it stops dead.

    This is the one action it can't undo, so it isn't allowed to take it alone. It has done the homework for you: what this touches, what it doesn't, and the one thing it flagged as needing a named human to agree — it touches regional payment routing.

    A person decides. Not as a policy we've written down somewhere. As a wall.

  7. 07

    Only the right person can sign

    Do
    Note the signing identity at the bottom of the gate, then click Approve & cryptographically sign.
    They see
    The gate closes and the record is sealed. Optional: before approving, switch the persona in the top right to one without signing rights — the same button refuses.

    Look at whose name is on this. The system knows who I am, and it knows I'm allowed to approve this particular kind of change. If I were somebody else, this button would refuse me — and I can show you that if it's useful.

    When I approve, it seals the record with a tamper-proof stamp. If anyone changes one character of what was decided, who decided it, or when — the stamp stops matching, and you know.

    So when your auditor asks "who approved this, and what exactly did they approve?", the answer isn't a meeting invitation and somebody's memory. It's a sealed record.

  8. 08

    It keeps watching after go-live

    Do
    Click Continuous Evolution, then Create remediation PR & test.
    They see
    A flagged security risk in a supplier component, and a fix drafted automatically.

    Most projects end at go-live. This is what happens for the two years afterwards, which is where the cost actually lives.

    It's still watching. It's found a piece of supplier software this system depends on that's now out of date and carries a security risk — and it has already written the fix and queued it for the same approval you just watched.

    Nobody filed a ticket. Nobody noticed on a Tuesday. That's the difference between software that decays and software that maintains itself.

  9. 09

    The regulator turns up

    Do
    Click Regulation in the top row. Wait for the scan — about two seconds. Let the red sit for a beat, then click Enforce control plane.
    They see
    A red verdict with named failures, which then turn green.

    Now imagine the regulator asks tomorrow morning. Not in six months — tomorrow.

    This checks the work against the European AI rules, the financial resilience rules, and data protection, and it tells you the truth: right now, some of it fails. That is deliberate. I'd rather show you a tool that reports honestly than one that always says green.

    And now — with the controls switched on, the same evidence answers the same questions, and it clears.

    The point isn't the green ticks. It's that you got an answer in two seconds, instead of three consultants and six weeks.

  10. 10

    Break it on purpose

    Do
    Click Inject drift in the top row.
    They see
    A red lockout banner. The permission is voided instantly and the path to production freezes.

    Let me try to cheat. I've just changed a file outside the boundary we agreed — the sort of thing that normally goes unnoticed for months.

    Permission revoked, immediately. Everything downstream frozen. Not a warning email somebody archives — the work simply stops until what's on disk matches what was agreed.

  11. 11

    Hand them the controls

    Do
    Click the MODEL box in the top right. Pick a public cloud option, point out the failure, then pick an air-gapped one on client hardware.
    They see
    With a public option chosen, the data-protection check fails. With the private option, it clears — and the cost line stops counting usage and starts counting hardware hours.

    Last thing, and this one is for whoever in your organisation has to sign off on where your data goes.

    You choose which AI does the work. Pick one running in somebody else's cloud, and watch — the data protection check fails, on the spot, because your customer data would leave your walls.

    Now pick this one: the same capability, running on hardware you own, disconnected from the internet. It clears. And notice the cost line changed — you're no longer renting by the word, you're using machines you've already paid for.

    I'm not asking you to take my word for where your data goes. Take the controls and change it yourself.

  12. 12

    Close, and reset

    Do
    Click Reset to return to the start for the next room.
    They see
    Everything back to baseline.

    So — same team, same work, a seventh of the cost. But that isn't really what I came to show you.

    What I came to show you is that every claim I just made, you watched the system prove on itself. It refused to start on bad data. It stopped before the irreversible step. It reported itself as non-compliant when it was. It caught me cheating.

The question you will be asked

What actually changes when you switch the AI

Stage 11 is the strongest ground you have, so it is worth knowing exactly what moves. Six things do, and the record notices all of them. The console's own help (?) shows this same list against whichever model is live at the time.

Where your data goes

The record's data_residency line changes — region, and whether the payload leaves your estate at all. This is the line a CISO reads first.

Whether it is even allowed

The data-protection check is re-run on the spot. A model in someone else's cloud fails it. Cleaning up your data does not change that — where it may travel is a separate question from whether it is tidy.

How you are billed

A hosted model charges by the word. A model on your own hardware charges by the hour of machine time you already pay for. The cost line on screen switches basis.

What level of assurance you are at

Four tiers, from a public API up to weights running air-gapped on client hardware. The tier is written into the record with the change.

Which model did the work

The exact model identifier is stamped on the record, so months later an auditor can see which one produced which change.

That you changed it at all

The switch is itself logged, with both models named and the new location. Changing the AI is a governed act here, not a settings tweak.

Keep in your pocket

If someone interrupts

"Is this real, or a mock-up?"

Straight answer: the scenario is prepared, the behaviour is real. The seal on the record is genuinely computed in front of you, the controls genuinely block, and you can drive it yourself right now.

"What if it makes a mistake?"

Then it's caught at one of the checkpoints you just watched, before anything irreversible happens — and the record shows exactly where. That's the whole design.

"Our data can't leave the country."

Go back to stage 11 and let them pick the option themselves. This is the strongest ground you have; don't rush past it.

Before a client meeting

Two things to check

Refresh the open-weight model names

The Claude entries in the model switcher are current first-party model names, context windows and prices. The open-weight entries are not from a verified source and that space moves fast. They sit in one commented block in src/data/models.ts — check them against the vendors before anyone technical is in the room.

Don't present an old download

Any copy of intelligent-flow-console.html saved earlier predates auto-play, the contextual help and the traceability spine — and, before that, a round of layout fixes. Delete it and press the download button above, which now saves the file properly instead of opening it in a tab.
